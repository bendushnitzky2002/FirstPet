# Hypotheses

> **Source of truth** (agreed 2026-10-22). Each app's screens, the landing page (`site/index.html`), the scope cards, and the deck follow this file. If a hypothesis changes, change it here first.

Each prototype tests one hypothesis for one persona. They must be different products, not the same product with a different color: A is for owners, B is for pet care professionals, C is for shelters.

Every prototype ends in a fake door: a paid option that does not exist yet, used to measure interest. The fake door rules in [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) apply to all three.

## Prototype A: Shared Check

**Persona:** Priya, a first-time co-owner watching her budget. She shares the decision with her partner, Marcus.

**Hypothesis:** FirstPet offers prospective owners a free readiness check they can share with their partner that will let them know whether they are ready to own a pet. After the results, at least 10% of users who completed the readiness check will select the "premium plan" option, which will tell us whether premium features are worth pursuing.

**What we want to learn:**

- Is the pre-adoption readiness check enough to convince users to sign up for a premium subscription after they have received their evaluation?

**How the prototype tests it:**

- The full readiness check is free, needs no account, and can be shared with a partner.
- At the end of the readiness workflow, a survey button asks whether the user would consider signing up for a premium subscription that offers post-adoption pet care assistance.

**What would prove it wrong:**

- Fewer than 10% of users who complete the readiness check choose the premium subscription.

**Measure:** of the users who reach the verdict, the share who choose the premium option.

## Prototype B: Pro Network

**Persona:** Dana, a partnership lead at a pet-related organization who wants to reach future pet owners early and be associated with responsible owners.

**Hypothesis:** Professionals will value a network that introduces them to prepared future pet owners, with the owner's plan shared by consent, and they will accept an annual membership for it rather than pay per lead.

**What we want to learn:**

- Is a pool of prepared first-time owners a more compelling customer acquisition source than buying leads one at a time?
- What do professionals want to see about a household before they say yes to an introduction?
- Does membership versus per-lead pricing change how they react?

**How the prototype tests it:**

- A professional-side dashboard with "Pending introductions": future owners with their readiness check, plus anything else they chose to share from their answers. The professional accepts or passes.
- The first page a professional lands on is "Your network profile," which shows what the vetting badge looks like to owners. There is no ranking and no paid placement anywhere.
- The main test is a membership page with two tiers (solo and clinic) and a "Join the network" button that records the click and asks one question: "What would make our service worth it?"

**What would prove it wrong:**

- Pet businesses would rather pay per transaction, in a pay-per-use format.

**Measure:** of the professionals who reach the membership page, the share who click "Join the network," and what they answer.

## Prototype C: Shelter Hand-off

**Persona:** Carla, adoption coordinator at a partner shelter, who wants to reduce the shelter's rate of returns.

**Hypothesis:** Shelters will make the readiness check the first step of their adoption process if it is free, and if the readiness check reaches the shelter before a prospective visit with all the information needed and flags anything that is missing. They will not pay per adoption, but may pay for analytics across adoptions.

**What we want to learn:**

- Is a shelter willing to pay for access to a network of recent adopters, plus a measure of and support for their readiness, if it could lower the shelter's return rate?

**How the prototype tests it:**

- A counselor dashboard with "Upcoming visits" showing each adopter's readiness summary and verdict, with anything missing flagged.
- A one-click "Request a check" link to send to an applicant.
- A check-in list with flagged responses.
- An upgrade option for "Analytics and multi-location" with a "Subscribe now" button and one question: "What would make our service worth it?"

**What would prove it wrong:**

- Shelters do not choose the premium option to get further analytics.

**Measure:** of the counselors who see the upgrade option, the share who click "Subscribe now," and what they answer.
